Start here: your first subtitle
  1. Check your media. Open and confirm your test files appear. The app uses your mapped media folder; start with one short sample.
  2. Download a speech model before processing audio. Open and download the model selected in Settings. Medium is selected by default; tiny is useful for a quick first test. Wait until the download job says completed and the model says INSTALLED. Selecting a model does not download it.
  3. Choose languages. In , choose the downloaded model, original audio language (or auto) and subtitle languages, then save. English-only output can use direct English translation without a language pack. Other targets need suitable packs from .
  4. Try one file. Queue a video from Media library, then check its job here. Use Preview or Download after it completes, or select the new subtitle track in Jellyfin after a library refresh. Check quality before enabling automatic watching.

Already have an SRT? Open , upload or select the file, choose its original and target languages, and install the required language packs. No speech model is needed for SRT translation, including translation into English.

Failed before downloading a model? After the download completes, click Retry on that failed video job. Old error messages remain in its history until retried. Skipped: requested subtitles already exist means a matching full subtitle track was detected. Choose another sample, or select Regenerate as a new version in Media library and queue that file to create another version while preserving the originals.

Synced lyrics for your music apps

Pick a song and Zilcho Subs listens to the singing and writes lyric files next to it, ready for apps that show lyrics while a song plays: Song.lrc (scrolls in time in Jellyfin and most music apps), Song.txt (plain lyrics) and, if turned on in Settings, Song.words.lrc (lights up word by word in karaoke-style players). Your music files are never changed, and lyrics you already have are never overwritten.

Singing is harder to recognize than talking, especially over loud music, so expect some wrong words. Fix them with Preview & edit once the job finishes. Larger speech models do noticeably better on songs.
